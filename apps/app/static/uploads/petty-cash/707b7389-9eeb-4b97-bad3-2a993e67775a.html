The one-line difference

AttendSmart knows who, not where. The camera knows where, not whose. AttendSmart gets a face 100–300 pixels wide but cannot prove the student is in the room. The camera guarantees a body in a seat but sees that face at 24–40 pixels and has no liveness check at all.

Scored on 15 criteria at equal weight they finish 46–48 — a dead heat. Three of five weightings favour the camera, two favour AttendSmart. There is no technically correct answer; there is a choice about which six criteria matter most to NIAT.

Three things to settle before any money is committed

1. Video or snapshots? The design was described as capturing a video of the class. Sai’s calculator models three still frames per period and says explicitly “not continuous video”. A 2-minute clip at 2 fps is 240 frames instead of 3 — roughly 80× the Rekognition volume, on the order of ₹28 crore a year at list rates. This gap is larger than everything else in the comparison.

2. Self-host the recognition. Renting it from AWS costs an extra ₹1 crore over three years and is 76% of the camera system’s running cost. The same conclusion applies to AttendSmart’s liveness check.

3. Ask security and facilities what they would pay. At a 30% co-funding share the camera premium drops from ₹79 L to ₹4 L, and the decision stops being about money. One conversation, not a study.

Cheapest way to remove the remaining uncertainty

Four of the six decisive facts are unmeasured, and five of the six cost nothing: survey whether laptops are actually present and charged in live sessions; count how many students miss the 10-second window; instrument one period to find the real peak load; ask about co-funding; fix what is already known broken in AttendSmart. The sixth costs ₹9,350 — buy one Prama varifocal camera and measure the actual pixels between a back-row student’s eyes. That single measurement settles whether a room needs two, three or four cameras, a swing of more than ₹15 lakh across the estate.

Contender A

AttendSmart

device-mediated · laptop-first · built

A QR and a code appear on the classroom screen. Students type the code within 10 seconds — the check-in, and the presence proof. The instructor clicks Start attendance, the camera opens on every checked-in device, and each student blinks twice against a 512-value enrolled signature. Designed around the mandatory college laptop; phone is a secondary path.

Contender B

Classroom camera

room-mediated · proposed, not built

Fixed cameras in each room recording continuously. Frames sampled across each period and matched against the same enrolled signatures. No student action, no device, no code.

Where the balance sits

AttendSmart carries more trade-offs, and they are structural. Code sharing, on-campus relay, IP and hotspot spoofing, check-in-then-leave, virtual-camera injection and device failure are six separate holes; only two close cheaply, and every genuine fix for the location problem is a piece of hardware in the room. The camera carries two: money, and a 7–11% miss rate. Both of those are manageable with process — a budget line and an appeals workflow.

The sharpest way to hold the difference: AttendSmart knows who, not where. The camera knows where, not whose. AttendSmart wins on price, running cost, scaling, compliance and deployment risk — it costs ₹79–89 lakh less over three years and adds new universities for nothing. The camera wins on presence, needing nothing from the student, zero effort for student and instructor, a richer record, and dual-use asset value. They draw on recall and failure blast radius.

03

The proxy ledger — what you have to let go

Fifteen distinct fraud methods. Neither system stops all of them. Hardened means AttendSmart with device binding, network scoping, corrected proxy logic and screen-replay detection — all ₹0 in hardware.

Rank the residuals by effort, not by cleverness

Attendance fraud is opportunistic. AttendSmart's survivors are the zero-effort, invisible ones — check in and walk out, or have a friend relay the code while you sit in the canteen. Those become normal within weeks. The camera's survivors need a photograph or a tablet propped on a desk in front of an instructor and sixty-nine classmates. Technically undefended, socially very hard. So the camera's residual list is longer on paper and smaller in practice; AttendSmart's is the reverse.

Choose AttendSmart — you let go of location

You can prove who. You cannot prove where.

  • Check-in-then-leave is entirely undefended and costs zero effort. Thirty seconds of a fifty-minute class.
  • Network scoping is a speed bump, not a control. Campus Wi-Fi cannot tell this room from the canteen, and the boundary itself is porous.
  • Virtual-camera injection is a laptop-specific risk a phone-first design would largely avoid.

Closable? Only by adding a room signal — periodic re-verification (friction), Bluetooth beacons (₹2.12 L), or a head-count camera (₹8.71 L). Every genuine fix is hardware in the room, which is the camera's argument arriving by another road.

Choose the camera — you let go of certainty about identity

You can prove a body was there. You cannot prove whose.

  • No liveness check of any kind. Not a configuration gap — no liveness technique works at 24–40 px, at any price.
  • Look-alike separation degrades sharply. Four confusable pairs were found at 104–156 px; at 24–40 px that rises steeply.
  • 7–11% of present students missed per frame — up to 107,869 wrongly-absent marks a month, each with no retry.

Closable? Partly. Continuous recording lets temporal analysis catch a face that never moves. More cameras raise the pixel count. Neither reaches AttendSmart's identity confidence.

Before quoting "zero misidentifications"

The pilot recorded no student marked present as somebody else across 64 attempts. That does not demonstrate what it appears to. The build under test was deliberately permissive so face attendance would pass — and a loose threshold makes false accepts more likely, not less; the exhaustive comparison found four confusable pairs scoring above the threshold in use. The blink check was not functioning (frames captured with eyes open), so the liveness stage the architecture depends on was never exercised, and the one attack tried against it succeeded. And 64 attempts is far too small — a system with a 1-in-200 error rate would be expected to show zero errors at that sample size.

What still stands: the physics favours AttendSmart, and that does not depend on the build. What does not: any claim that this implementation has proven it. Both systems now stand on unvalidated implementations.

Reconciling the "under $1 per student" estimate

The internal estimate and this report are measuring different things and both are right. Self-hosted, they are close: ₹80–126 per student per year is $0.91–1.43 once development and maintenance are counted — so the under-a-dollar figure holds only at the cloud-autoscaled end, and only for AWS run-cost as his sheet defines it. The ₹1.43 crore figure is not what we would pay — it is what a commercial per-check liveness API would charge, and appears here only as the option to reject. The arithmetic, so it can be checked: AWS Face Liveness at $0.015 × 1,320 checks = $19.80 per student per year at the headline rate, or $21.82 once the tiered volume discount is applied across the estate. Landing under $1 while checking every session needs $0.00076 per check — twenty times cheaper than AWS — or self-hosting, which is what both estimates assume. Liveness was never the deciding cost. Peak GPU is, and neither estimate had priced it.

Raise with Sai before anything is committed

1. Video or snapshots? The design was described as capturing a video of the class with everyone present. The calculator models three still frames per period per camera and states plainly that a snapshot is “a single still frame pulled from the camera feed at one moment — not continuous video”. These are not the same product. A 2-minute clip sampled at 2 fps is 240 frames per camera per session instead of 3 — roughly 80× the Rekognition volume, which at list rates is on the order of ₹28 crore a year. The gap between the two readings is larger than the entire rest of this brief.

2. Self-host the matching. ₹1 crore over three years is the price of the convenience. The same ArcFace pipeline on an owned GPU has no per-call fee, and the enrolled signatures are already ours.

3. VPN topology is still open in the thread. One shared tunnel is ₹0.39 L a year; eighteen per-campus connections are ₹6.94 L. Revanth reports the Pune campus already runs over VGW with no additional transfer charge, which suggests the cheaper answer is available — worth closing before it is designed the other way.

4. The hardware still has to be paid for. His calculator deliberately excludes cameras and NVRs. At 3 cameras per room, with the four drives per campus that 9.5 TB of monthly footage actually needs, that is ₹59.4 lakh of capital that no line in the AWS estimate covers.

Two hybrid configurations

Hybrid 1 — device-primary + presence cross-check

AttendSmart hardened, plus one 2 MP fixed dome per room used only to count occupied seats — no face template extracted from room video, no video retained. If AttendSmart records 38 present and the room holds 31 bodies, the session is flagged.

Cost: ₹8,218/room = ₹8.71 L capital, ₹1.22 L/yr. Three-year total with AttendSmart: ₹23.5–31.3 L (₹105–140 per student/yr).

Wins: closes the location gap with an independent physical signal at a quarter of the camera's cost. Head-counting needs DORI Detection (25 PPM), not Identification (250), so none of the pixel problem applies. Smallest privacy footprint of any configuration. Loses: keeps the device dependency, the 10-second window and instructor involvement; gains no duration data or dual-use value; two systems to maintain.

Hybrid 2 — camera-primary + device fallback

The full camera estate as the default, with AttendSmart retained as the exception route for students the camera could not resolve — occluded, turned away, or badly seated.

Cost: both estates. ₹98.4–106.2 L over three years, falling to roughly ₹55–75 L with a 30–50% dual-use allocation.

Wins: the highest achievable recall of any configuration, because the two failure modes are genuinely uncorrelated — occlusion does not affect a webcam, a dead laptop does not affect a camera. Zero student effort for the ~90% the camera resolves, and the 10-second window stops being a single point of failure. Loses: most expensive, largest privacy footprint, two systems to reconcile, hardest compliance position, and the most complex student experience to explain.

On hybrids generally: both buy real robustness and both cost more than the single system they extend. Neither should be chosen before measurements 1–3 in Section 07 exist — a hybrid designed around an unmeasured failure rate is a guess with a bigger budget.

Who is actually certified — and who is not

STQC certifies the camera. There is no certification for attendance software.

MeitY notified the Essential Requirements for Security of CCTV Cameras (ER:01) by Gazette in April 2024 under the Compulsory Registration Order. Network cameras must comply from 9 April 2025, with full enforcement from 1 April 2026 — non-conforming cameras cannot legally be sold. Two routes test the same six requirements: BIS ER-01 registration or STQC IoTSCS. The six: no default passwords, encrypted video over TLS/HTTPS, secure boot with signed firmware, disabled debug and test ports, supply-chain transparency on chipset origin, and a published vulnerability disclosure policy. Government procurement has required it since 6 June 2024. The practical effect is that Hikvision, Dahua and other non-compliant brands are out of the Indian market.

What it does not cover: certification is a cybersecurity bar for the device. It says nothing about whether a face-attendance system is accurate. Every classroom attendance product is a certified camera plus an uncertified software layer.

What this means for procurement

There is no certified end-to-end classroom attendance product in India. The certified vendors sell doorway terminals; the classroom vendors sell software that is not certified and publishes no accuracy evidence. Whatever is chosen, NIAT would be integrating a certified camera with an unproven recognition layer — which is an argument for keeping recognition in-house, where the threshold, the audit trail and the failure behaviour are ours to control.

Two specification traps

1. "Identify at 11 m" is not a face-recognition figure. DORI Identification is 250 pixels per metre, which is 15.8 pixels between the eyes — one sixth of the ISO/IEC 39794‑5 minimum of 90 px and half the practical floor for a deep model. Whatever Identify distance appears on a quotation, treat usable recognition distance as a small fraction of it.

2. Do not budget from MRP. Consumer listings show 48–57% MRP-to-street gaps. The prices above are GST-inclusive channel-partner prices — a defensible floor, and institutional volume at 200+ units should land at or below them.

Demand in writing from every vendor
  • Exact focal range for that model and firmware, from the manufacturer, not a retailer.
  • Measured pixel count between a subject's eyes at 6 m and 9 m at full zoom, in a room, on video.
  • RTSP URL format, with written confirmation that direct stream access works without routing through the vendor's own VMS.
  • Sustained frame rate at full resolution, and whether it drops when analytics are enabled.
  • STQC certificate number for that model and firmware version.
  • Bitrate at H.265+ in a low-motion indoor scene — this is what sizes the storage bill.
Which to buy first

Buy one Prama 4 MP varifocal dome at ₹9,350 and measure — and get CP-UNC-TG81ZL12-VMD quoted in parallel. It is the cheapest verified unit with motorised zoom, which is the one control that lets you trade field of view against pixel density without a site visit. Mount it front-corner at 2.5 m, tilt 15–20°, zoom to full telephoto, pull an RTSP frame and count the pixels between a student's eyes at 6 m and 9 m in a real 70-seat room. That single ₹9,350 measurement settles whether the room needs two, three or four cameras — a swing of more than ₹15 lakh across 106 classrooms, and 0.02% of the camera option's capital to find out.

The CP Plus long-throw unit is the one that could change the whole comparison. At full telephoto it puts 102 px between a back-row student's eyes at 9 m — above the ISO 90 px minimum, which nothing else in this brief reaches. The catch is real: at that zoom each camera covers only 2.37 m, so a 10 m back row needs five, and the published close-focus distance at telephoto is 6 m, so those cameras cannot serve the front rows at all. There is no public price. If it quotes anywhere near the Prama, the camera option gets materially stronger on identity and materially more expensive at the same time.

06

Compliance and the adoption record

DPDP — the two provisions that decide this

The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025; obligations on notice, retention, security and children's data come into force 14 May 2027. A child is anyone under 18 — stricter than GDPR or COPPA — and NIAT's first-year intake includes 17-year-olds. Section 9(1) requires verifiable parental consent: applies to both options. Section 9(3) prohibits tracking or behavioural monitoring of children outright and cannot be unlocked by consent: this is where the options diverge. Rule 12 gives educational institutions a conditional exemption — narrow, purpose-bound, requiring documented necessity. Not a blanket permission for either.

Required either way: a DPIA, a written retention schedule, a documented lawful basis for under-18s, and a per-university notice. Every enforcement case reviewed turned on a missing impact assessment, not on the technology.

What this actually tells us — and it cuts against the earlier reading

The world’s largest face-attendance deployments are A2, not A1. Telangana and Karnataka both work by a teacher photographing the class — many faces at distance. That is the same physics as a fixed camera, just handheld and closer. So the “tens of thousands of schools” precedent does not validate AttendSmart’s architecture; it validates the many-faces-at-distance approach that the camera option also uses.

That softens the physics objection to the camera. Telangana processes 2.1 million facial templates a day in production. Recognising many faces at once, at classroom distance, evidently works well enough for a state government to run it at national scale — which is close to the thing this brief called unproven.

It does not eliminate the objection. A2 is handheld at 2–5 m and the teacher can move, rotate and re-shoot; a fixed camera at 6–12 m cannot. That is the difference between 40–90 px and 24–40 px. And Telangana records ~70% daily attendance, which is an attendance figure, not a recall figure — nobody has published how much of the gap is genuine absence and how much is the system failing to find a present student.

The practical consequence: A2 is a third option NIAT has not evaluated. An instructor photographing the room from the front on a phone or tablet needs no fixed hardware, no student device, no 10-second code and no cabling — and it has the largest body of real-world evidence behind it of anything in this brief. It should be costed alongside the other two before a decision is taken.

What this comparison does not know

Laptop availability and the 10-second miss rate have never been surveyed. No pixel measurement exists from a real NIAT classroom — Section 01's figures are lens arithmetic from published standards. Neither option's recall has been measured at NIAT, and the two figures quoted are not measured the same way; the multi-frame sampling gain for the camera is real and unquantified. Two cameras per room is unproven and at 70 students in a 9–10 m room, three or four is more likely. Room width is derived, not measured. The 8 MP Prama price is inferred from a verified 4 MP sibling; the Matrix price is deliberately blank. Installation at ₹3,500 per camera comes from published ranges, not a site survey — 18 campuses is 18 sets of site conditions. Exception-handling labour is unpriced on both sides. Sai Kiran's calculator is now incorporated, but it models three still snapshots per period while the design was described as a video — not the same product, and unresolved. The scores are judgements — disagreeing with one is a legitimate way to disagree with this brief, and the weights are yours to change. No head-to-head study exists anywhere comparing both architectures on the same cohort.
